The person behind Naabi Kamalam

DS

Naabi Kamalam exists for people who are functioning fine on the outside but feel stuck, noisy, or reactive on the inside — in a relationship, a career decision, a confidence gap, or just a general sense of "not myself." No diagnosis or crisis is required to start — just a willingness to look at the pattern honestly, and someone to look at it with you.

My Story

My path to this work wasn't a straight line. It came from twelve years in corporate IT, and from growing up in a traditional Indian family navigating a Western world.

I moved often growing up — for work, for marriage — raised inside diversity from childhood. Different cities, different expectations of who I was supposed to be in each one. You learn to read a room fast when the room keeps changing.

Along the way came achievements, leadership roles — rooms full of people I was responsible for. And it was there, watching how people responded under pressure, that a question started following me: why do people behave so differently than I expect them to?

Underneath it all, the weight just felt like stress — the kind most of us carry so quietly we forget to name it.

Then came a health struggle that stopped me and asked me to look at everything honestly. What it gave back was trust — in the process of my own life, and a faith in something larger than my own certainty.

And one decision changed everything: I stopped blaming the system, the culture, the people around me, and instead looked at the whole picture clearly and honestly. Not as a victim of circumstance, but as someone willing to rise and see it for what it was.

That clarity built something real — self-earned confidence. And underneath it, eventually, peace.

My academic background is in physics and computer science. Physics gave me superposition — many possibilities can exist at once, until you choose. It gave me entanglement, too — a name for the threads that keep us bound to old patterns long after we've outgrown them, and the reminder that they can be gently traced and undone. Computer science gave me another lens: the subconscious as a running program, replaying the same loop until something interrupts it — a loop most of us never notice, and just call "stress."

All of it — the moving, the leading, the illness, the faith, the science — is what shapes how I coach you now. Not borrowed theory. A framework built from having actually lived the tangle, and found a way through it.
